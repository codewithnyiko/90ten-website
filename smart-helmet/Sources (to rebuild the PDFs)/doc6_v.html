How the helmet talks to a server

In plain words

Think of a child who must hand a note to a teacher every morning. The child's card says: "First go to the head office and ask which teacher to give it to." The head office answers with a name and the child hands over the note.

That is how the helmet works now. The head office is the address server and the teacher is the server it reports to. Both are the supplier's, as far as we can tell.

Idea 1: we change the card so the head office is ours. The child still asks first and our office says: "Give it to our teacher."
Idea 2: we write our teacher's name straight on the card. The child goes to our teacher and asks nobody. We do not know if the helmet can do this, so we ask.

Is the helmet forced to use their server?

We do not know yet. This is what the Interface Document says (pp3 and 4).

Way 1: change the addresses on their platform

The helmet still asks their address server first. We only change the answer it gets, in their control platform. We would still depend on them.

Way 2: change the address inside the helmet

The helmet asks our address server instead and we run it. The document says this works only on helmets with a camera and that a helmet without one needs special software put on it. It does not explain how. The two methods it mentions, "Modification Method 1 and 2", are never described.

What "their API" means and why we avoid it

API

Think of a menu in a restaurant. You can only order what is on the menu and the kitchen gives you what you ordered. An API is a menu for a computer: a list of what you can ask it and what it gives back.

Their API

Their ready-made server and its menu. If we use it, we depend on their server and on how they run it. We want to avoid that.

The messages the helmet speaks

This is the other thing and it is what we need. It is the list of what the helmet says (where I am, I need help) and what it understands (take a photo). If we have the full list, any server can understand the helmet, including ours.

SDK

A starter box that comes with the list, like a LEGO set with the pieces and the instructions. It helps us build faster. If they do not have one we will build our own API on our server and give it a version and a date, so everyone can see when something changes.

What we need from the supplier

1. The steps to change the address inside the helmet ourselves or to make it report straight to our server, for each helmet we buy.

2. The full list of messages between the helmet and a server, with examples. If they have no online version we will keep our own, with a version and date.

3. A promise that nothing else needs their servers to work.

How we think it should work

Each mine is different, but the idea is the same. A beacon says where the worker is, the helmet notes it with the time and sends it to our server when it has signal and the control room sees the trail.

Mine 1: mobile signal at the shaft and a few Wi-Fi points

The control room sees

The trail from the last Wi-Fi point or from the surface, with the time of each beacon.

What is lost

Live position between the Wi-Fi points. The trail arrives in chunks.

Other kinds of mine

Mine 2: no Wi-Fi at all

The control room sees

The full trail once the worker reaches signal at the shaft top. If a worker does not come out, only what was sent before the signal was lost.

What is lost

Live position underground. The trail of a missing worker stays on the helmet.

Depends on

Questions 5 and 6.

Mine 3: Wi-Fi all along the tunnels

The control room sees

A live trail and live video, because the helmet is always connected.

What is lost

Very little. The cost is building and keeping the Wi-Fi.

Depends on

Questions 1, 10 and 11.

A mine with a face zone

Equipment is kept about 50 m from blasting, so the area at the face has no beacons or Wi-Fi. A helmet that is not seen means unknown, not clear.

Mine 4: beacons and Wi-Fi up to the edge of the face zone

The control room sees

The worker last seen at the beacon on the edge of the face zone, then nothing until they walk back. A warning if they enter the zone at blast time.

What is lost

Everything inside the face zone. We know where the worker was last seen, not where they are.

Depends on

Questions 5, 6 and 7.

Why this matters

The answers to questions 4 to 7 decide which of these mines the helmet can serve and how much we must add, such as more beacons or more Wi-Fi points.
