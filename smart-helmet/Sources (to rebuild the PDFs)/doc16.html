Screen 1: Overview

The first thing the control room sees.

  1. Four numbers at the top. Workers on shift means signed in with a helmet.
  2. The site map. Each filled circle is a worker and a ring is an alarm. Positions come from the helmet GPS.
  3. The alarms list. The newest is at the top.

Screen 2: Sign in

At the lamp room we link a worker to a helmet for this shift. The link is our own record.

  1. Find the worker by name or employee number. We load the worker list once from the mine.
  2. Type or scan the helmet number. The screen shows if the helmet is seen now and its battery. Both come from the helmet report (pp15 to 18). The report also says if the helmet is worn so we can show that too.
  3. Pick the shift and the section.
  4. Sign in links this worker and this helmet for this shift. Return ends the link when the helmet comes back.
  5. Everyone signed in today. The same helmet can go to another worker on the next shift and the history keeps who had it.

Screen 3: Shift view

Who is seen and who is not. This is where the control room finds problems early.

  1. Pick the shift.
  2. Workers signed in whose helmet reported in the last 5 minutes. A worn check shows a helmet that is not on the head.
  3. Workers signed in whose helmet has not reported. We show the last place and the time. Underground the helmet may simply have no signal so this is a prompt to check and not an alarm.
  4. A helmet that reports but nobody is signed in to it. We can link it to a worker on the spot.
  5. Workers who handed their helmet back.

Screen 4: Headcount

For a muster or an evacuation. It shows everyone signed in by where they were last seen.

  1. The numbers and a button to print the list.
  2. Groups by the place each worker was last seen. The last group is people we have not seen.
  3. The workers in the chosen group with the time they were last seen.
  4. A supervisor ticks each person as safe at the muster point.

Screen 5: One worker

Pick a worker to see their day and to send the helmet a command.

  1. A list of workers with a search box. A filled dot means the helmet is seen.
  2. Where the worker has been today with the time.
  3. The same route on the map.
  4. Commands the helmet understands: take a photo and start or stop recording and play a message and join a Wi-Fi network (pp27 and 25 and 42).

Screen 6: Alarm and last seen

What opens when an alarm comes in.

  1. The alarm bar with two buttons.
  2. The last seen card with place and time and battery.
  3. Who is close to the worker by GPS. The dashed box shows what comes later.
  4. The way the worker came on the map.

Screen 7: Helmets and settings

Which helmets may log in and how each one behaves.

  1. Add a helmet by its device number.
  2. Only helmets in the list can log in. The document has no password so this is our own check (p10). The list also shows who each helmet is issued to.
  3. Settings from the helmet start-up reply: alarm temperature and helmet off delay and no movement time and picture quality and beacon scanning and positioning and video upload (pp6 and 7).
  4. The helmet reads these when it switches on so a change reaches it at the next start-up (p3).

Screen 8: Bluetooth test

Every Bluetooth device the helmets hear. This is how we find out which beacons work.

  1. The tabs. The beacons tab is for the next version.
  2. Every Bluetooth device the helmets hear with signal strength (p15).
  3. How we use it: put a real beacon beside a helmet and see if it shows up.

Screen 9: Photos and videos

Everything the helmets send us as files. This needs our file server so it comes in version 3.

  1. Two tabs: photos and the videos stored on helmets.
  2. Photos the helmets took with the worker and the time. Each photo also carries a position (p30).
  3. Videos stay on the helmet until we ask. We see the list and press Send to us (pp31 to 33).

Screen 10: Updates

Send new software to chosen helmets. It needs the answers to questions 8 and 9 first.

  1. The new software: a version and a download address and a check number (pp5 and 8).
  2. Each helmet and the software it runs now. The helmet tells us this when it asks for its settings (p3).
  3. Tick one helmet to test first. We can answer each helmet on its own.
  4. Send. The helmet gets the update the next time it starts. Whether it accepts our software is not known yet.
